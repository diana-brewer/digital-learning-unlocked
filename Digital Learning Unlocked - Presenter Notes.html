Flow Note

The session is build-first: three short demos the audience plays along with (static case, dynamic case, notebook), a table conversation between them, then one long build block at the end where everyone makes a real thing and shares it out. The fidelity slides keep their classic Think · Pair · Share.

Pacing tip

This is a fast-paced 60-minute session. The 1-minute slides are targets: if you're running behind, trim them to 30–45 seconds rather than cutting demo play time. Protect the build block above all; it can flex from 20 to 25 minutes (end the share-out early or let it ride into the buffer). If everything runs long, the buffer absorbs it.

Slide 5Demo 1: AI Builds the Static Case0:06–0:11 (5 min)
Speaker says

"Watch once, then play. [On screen: new chat, upload the six case files, paste Prompt 1 from Section 2.] Instead of building this in H5P, AI writes the case as one HTML file: a complete branching scenario. Look how much the prompt specifies: the structure, the reasoning box at every decision, the debrief screen, even the button that downloads the student's results as a text file for the instructor. The more you give the chatbot, the less you leave to chance."

"Now play the finished version, linked in Section 2. You're the student: click through, type your reasoning at each decision, and at the end press Download results for your instructor. That file is what you'd receive back: not just what they chose, but how they reasoned. Static means every learner sees the same case with the same published numbers."

Audience does

Plays the pre-built static case as a student for a few minutes; downloads their results file on the debrief screen and sees what the instructor receives.

Slide 6Demo 2: The Same Case, Live and Grounded0:11–0:16 (5 min)
Speaker says

"Same 19-year-old, level it up: dynamic and grounded. Everyone: new chat, upload the same six files, paste Prompt 2 from Section 3. [Wait for the room.] Now you're the student: introduce yourself, take the history, examine, order what you need. Ask for labs, you get the file's values, not inventions. Perform the exam, you get the published findings."

"When you're done, type encounter over. The debrief is built into the same prompt: five domains, rated Novice through Proficient, each with evidence from what you actually did. Audience does

Uploads the six files, pastes Prompt 2, runs the encounter as the student. Most reach at least a few turns; faster tables reach the debrief.

Watch for

Login walls and upload friction on phones: pair phone-only attendees with a laptop neighbor, or have them read your shared conversation link instead.

Slide 7Table Talk: Static or Dynamic?0:16–0:18 (2 min)
Speaker says

"Two minutes with your table. You just met the same case twice: static, every learner clicks the same path; dynamic, every learner runs it differently. When would you reach for the static version, and when does the dynamic one win? Think standardization, grading, replay, feedback."

Take one or two answers, then name it: "Static standardizes and makes grading clean. Dynamic practices the encounter itself and feedback lands on the reasoning. Same case, same files, different teaching moments."

Audience does

Two-minute table conversation on static versus dynamic uses; one or two answers shared aloud.

Slide 9The Fidelity Spectrum0:26–0:27 (1 min)
Speaker says

Walk left to right: "Text and audio, free or low. Image-based, free or $25. Screen-based, free trials. AR on your phone, free or $25. Immersive VR, $100 to $600. The question is never 'what's the best technology?' It's 'what matches my learning goal and my budget?'"

Say it explicitly, once, out loud: "If you flip ahead to Section 6, that one is your take-home. VR hardware, pricing, the decision framework, and the starter stacks from $0 to about $1,100 are all written out there. We will not walk them live today, but it is written to be used Monday morning."

Then add personally: "In our experience the reasoning lives at the text-and-audio end, images included: ECGs, X-rays, CTs, labs. The uncanny valley of avatar-based tools is real, near-human avatars distract from the very reasoning you're assessing. If I had to pick one tool from today's list for reasoning practice, it's the text-and-audio one from the conflict-of-interest slide: Sapient Clinician. Judge it on its merits."

Audience does

Sees the finished pericarditis case, a runnable demo in the guide, Section 5.

Slide 10Think · Pair · Share: Match the Tool0:27–0:29 (2 min)
Speaker says

"With your table group, think of a clinical skill your fellows need. Which point on the fidelity spectrum? Would text and audio do the job? Remember: text-and-audio cases include images: ECGs, X-rays, CTs, lab panels. Some text-and-audio tools already do this well. Would AR add something those images don't? Or does this genuinely require VR? Share your reasoning, and what the free option would be."

Audience does

Discusses in table groups for 2 minutes. They have been building all session; this is the one classic Think · Pair · Share.

Time check

You should be at ~0:29. Transition to the build block now.

Slide 11The Build Block: Make One Real Thing0:29–0:52 (23 min)
Speaker says

"You've watched every tool do its job. Now: make one real thing. Pick your build. Your own static case, Prompt 1 with any published case. Your own dynamic case, Prompt 2 with the same files. Something from a notebook: the AML sources, or any notebook you make, a quiz, flashcards, an audio overview. Or one of the take-home prompts: journal club, a teaching session, a procedure pre-brief, reflective writing, every one with the feedback built in."

"Stuck on the prompt? The Prompt Builder worksheet in Section 7 assembles it for you as you fill in the blanks. You have until 0:52, minus a few minutes to share. Build with your table or solo, I'll circulate. Go."

Audience does (23 min)

Picks a build and makes it: a case, a notebook artifact, or a take-home prompt product. Uses the Prompt Builder worksheet if starting from scratch. Works solo or with their table.

Speaker does (during the 23 min)

Circulate among tables. Coach stuck tables with starting points ("use the MedEdPORTAL files, or the AML notebook's sources"). Push on detail: "What's missing from your prompt? Give the chatbot more: rules, order, feedback." Watch for account walls and pair people up. Project a visible countdown.

Pick diverse builds: one case, one notebook artifact, one take-home prompt product.

Audience does

Three or four people share what they built. Others listen and ask questions.

Key Messages to Reinforce

The Five Things Attendees Should Remember
  1. You don't need to be a technology expert. You need to be a clinician who knows what your learners need.
  2. Ground AI in your source material. Upload your guidelines. Say "base your response only on the attached documents." NotebookLM does this for free.
  3. The more you give the chatbot, the better. Roles, rules, output order, citations, feedback: one detailed prompt does the whole job, as every prompt in the guide shows.
  4. Simulation is a spectrum. Text-and-audio is not "less than" VR, it's different. Match the fidelity to the learning goal.
  5. Start with one tool, one need, one small experiment. You don't need a grant or an IT ticket. You need to know what your learners need, and you already know that.
