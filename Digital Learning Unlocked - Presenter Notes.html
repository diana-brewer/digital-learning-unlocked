Flow Note

Attendees do Think · Pair · Share after each section (Obj 1, Obj 2, Obj 3) to 2 minutes each. The Capstone Design Sprint at the end is not the first time they're planning, it's an extension of the mini-discussions they've been having all along.

Pacing tip

This is a fast-paced 60-minute session. The 1-minute slides are targets, if you're running behind, trim them to 30–45 seconds rather than cutting a demo or a Think · Pair · Share. The last 3 minutes are buffer; if you're on time, use them for Q&A.

Slide 8The Elephant: Nobody Wrote This Case0:11–0:13 (2 min)
Speaker says

"The ungrounded case felt realistic, and that's exactly the risk. You can't know whether the details are right. No source, no citation, and every learner who runs it gets a different, unreviewed case. Would you sign your name to it?"

"And it scales: an ungrounded treatment update invents dosing. An ungrounded procedure pre-brief scrambles step order. An ungrounded handout goes home with a real patient. The pitfall scales with the stakes, and in education, an error propagates to every learner who meets it. Objective 2 is how we get realism AND verifiability."

Audience does

Looks at the "Beyond the Case" table in the guide (Section 4), eight curriculum formats, eight pitfalls, eight grounding sources.

Slide 11Live Demo: The Same Case, Grounded0:15–0:20 (5 min)
Frame it honestly

Grounding isn't a NotebookLM trick. Every major assistant works from your documents now. NotebookLM is just the easiest dedicated example to show live, and it's already loaded.

Have backup screenshots ready

If WiFi fails or the live demo breaks, switch to pre-loaded screenshots, or a 90-second screen recording of the same flow. The audience should never see you sweat.

Demo script (5 min)
  1. (30 sec) Ask ChatGPT to "create a case of acute pericarditis with ECG findings" WITHOUT uploading sources. Point out any inaccuracies.
  2. (1 min) Open NotebookLM (pre-loaded with pericarditis guideline PDF). Ask the same question. Show how responses cite specific passages.
  3. (1 min) Ask NotebookLM to create a clinical reasoning exercise + feedback rubric. Show how each element traces to the source.
  4. (Optional 60-sec variant) Frame it with the running example: upload the MedEdPORTAL case files 1 + 2 (download links: Guide → Section 4; NotebookLM: notebooklm.google.com) and ask "What four clinical states does this case move through?", grounding in someone else's published work, not your own files. That's the whole trick: go get the published material, then wire your teaching to it.
  5. (1 min) Click "Audio Overview" in NotebookLM. Play 15 seconds of the AI-generated podcast.
  6. (30 sec) "I just created a grounded, source-cited clinical reasoning case and feedback rubric in under 5 minutes, with near-zero hallucination risk because the AI worked only from my material. The exercise requires the learner to think and reason, not just pick an answer."

Slide 13Objective 3: Simulation Spectrum0:24–0:25 (1 min)
Speaker says

"Simulation is not just VR headsets. It's a spectrum, from text and audio through immersive VR. The most effective simulation matches the technology to the learning goal."

Slide 14The Fidelity Spectrum0:25–0:26 (1 min)
Speaker says

Walk left to right: "Text and audio, free or low. Image-based, free or $25. Screen-based, free trials. AR on your phone, free or $25. Immersive VR, $100 to $600. The question is never 'what's the best technology?' It's 'what matches my learning goal and my budget?'"

Then add personally: "In our experience the reasoning lives at the text-and-audio end, images included: ECGs, X-rays, CTs, labs. The uncanny valley of avatar-based tools is real, near-human avatars distract from the very reasoning you're assessing. If I had to pick one tool from today's list for reasoning practice, it's the text-and-audio one from the conflict-of-interest slide: Sapient Clinician. Judge it on its merits."

Audience does

Sees the finished pericarditis case, the same disease the notebook just grounded, as a runnable demo in the guide, Section 5.

Slide 15Think · Pair · Share: Match the Tool0:26–0:28 (2 min)

Slide 16Capstone Design Sprint0:28–0:29 (1 min intro, then 20 min work to 0:49)
Speaker says

"You've been discussing tools and ideas with your table group after each section. Now let's turn one of those ideas into a plan you can take back to your program. This isn't the first time you've been planning today, you've been doing mini-design thinking all along. Now you'll extend that into a complete implementation plan."

Walk through the 3 steps on the slide: prepare, upload, ask. "1. Identify a need. 2. Define learning goals. 3. Select a tool from the tables. 4. Map pedagogy to technology, why this tool? 5. Sketch your implementation plan. Pick the idea from your earlier discussions that feels most feasible and most useful. Scroll to Section 7 on the Workshop Guide, you'll find a fillable worksheet. I'll circulate to help."

Then: "You have 20 minutes. Go."

Audience does (20 min)

Works in table groups. Fills in the worksheet on their phones. Uses the tool tables in the Workshop Guide to select tools. Discusses, decides, writes.

Speaker does (during the 17 min)

Circulate among tables. Coach stuck groups with seed ideas ("opioid stewardship conversations," "end-of-life discussions," "pediatric dosing"). Push back on cost ("Can this be done with a free tool?"). Watch for the VR trap. Project a visible countdown and announce time checks at 5, 10, and 15 minutes.

Key Messages to Reinforce

The Five Things Attendees Should Remember
  1. You don't need to be a technology expert. You need to be a clinician who knows what your learners need.
  2. Ground AI in your source material. Upload your guidelines. Say "base your response only on the attached documents." NotebookLM does this for free.
  3. Authentic assessment, not MCQs. Use AI to create clinical reasoning cases, simulated encounters, and reflective prompts that require the learner to demonstrate their thinking.
  4. Simulation is a spectrum. Text-and-audio is not "less than" VR, it's different. Match the fidelity to the learning goal.
  5. Start with one tool, one need, one small experiment. You don't need a grant or an IT ticket. You need to know what your learners need, and you already know that.
