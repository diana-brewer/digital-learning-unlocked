Flow Note

The middle of the session is hands-on: attendees build Demo 1 and run Demo 2 themselves, with short table debriefs after each. Section 5 keeps its classic Think · Pair · Share. The Capstone Design Sprint at the end extends that pattern into a full implementation plan.

Pacing tip

This is a fast-paced 60-minute session. The 1-minute slides are targets, if you're running behind, trim them to 30–45 seconds rather than cutting a demo or a Think · Pair · Share. The last 6 minutes are buffer: protect the capstone, and extend it to 25 minutes if the room is moving; if you're on time, use them for Q&A.

Slide 4The 2×2 Map0:04–0:05 (1 min)
Speaker says

"Here is the map for the whole session. Two questions about any AI teaching tool. One: is it static, built once and fixed, or dynamic, regenerated every time you open it? Two: is it grounded, tied to a real cited source, or ungrounded, invented from nothing? We visit three quadrants together today. You build a static, grounded case. You run a dynamic, ungrounded one. And we finish in the payoff quadrant: dynamic and grounded."

Audience does

Sees the map on screen and in Section 1 of the guide. Each demo opens by naming its quadrant, so the map gets filled in as the session progresses.

Audience does

Slide 7The Elephant, Live: Debrief0:16–0:18 (2 min)
Speaker says

"The elephant, live: fast, engaging, and nobody wrote this case. Two questions for your table. What did you notice was different between your case and your neighbor's? And what would that mean for grading students on this?"

"Take one or two answers, then land it: every run is a fresh case with fresh numbers, and you cannot evaluate learners uniformly on data that is not uniform. Dynamic, yes. Grounded, no. Hold that thought: the fix is one quadrant away."

Audience does

Two-minute table debrief on the differences they saw; one or two answers shared aloud.

Slide 9Section 5: Simulation Spectrum0:23–0:24 (1 min)
Speaker says

"Simulation is not just VR headsets. It's a spectrum, from text and audio through immersive VR. The most effective simulation matches the technology to the learning goal."

Slide 10The Fidelity Spectrum0:24–0:25 (1 min)
Speaker says

Walk left to right: "Text and audio, free or low. Image-based, free or $25. Screen-based, free trials. AR on your phone, free or $25. Immersive VR, $100 to $600. The question is never 'what's the best technology?' It's 'what matches my learning goal and my budget?'"

Say it explicitly, once, out loud: "If you flip ahead to Section 6, that one is your take-home. VR hardware, pricing, the decision framework, and the starter stacks from $0 to about $1,100 are all written out there. We will not walk them live today, but it is written to be used Monday morning."

Then add personally: "In our experience the reasoning lives at the text-and-audio end, images included: ECGs, X-rays, CTs, labs. The uncanny valley of avatar-based tools is real, near-human avatars distract from the very reasoning you're assessing. If I had to pick one tool from today's list for reasoning practice, it's the text-and-audio one from the conflict-of-interest slide: Sapient Clinician. Judge it on its merits."

Audience does

Sees the finished pericarditis case, the same disease the notebook just grounded, as a runnable demo in the guide, Section 5.

Slide 11Think · Pair · Share: Match the Tool0:25–0:27 (2 min)
Speaker says

"With your table group, think of a clinical skill your fellows need. Which point on the fidelity spectrum? Would text and audio do the job? Remember: text-and-audio cases include images: ECGs, X-rays, CTs, lab panels. Some text-and-audio tools already do this well. Would AR add something those images don't? Or does this genuinely require VR? Share your reasoning, and what the free option would be."

Audience does

Discusses in table groups for 2 minutes. Tries the clinical reasoning case on their phone. This is their third TPS, they've been building ideas throughout.

Time check

You should be at ~0:34. Transition to the capstone workshop now.

Slide 12Capstone Design Sprint0:27–0:28 (1 min intro, then 20 min work to 0:48)
Speaker says

"You've been discussing tools and ideas with your table group after each section. Now let's turn one of those ideas into a plan you can take back to your program. This isn't the first time you've been planning today, you've been doing mini-design thinking all along. Now you'll extend that into a complete implementation plan."

Walk through the 3 steps on the slide: prepare, upload, ask. "1. Identify a need. 2. Define learning goals. 3. Select a tool from the tables. 4. Map pedagogy to technology, why this tool? 5. Sketch your implementation plan. Pick the idea from your earlier discussions that feels most feasible and most useful. Scroll to Section 7 on the Workshop Guide, you'll find a fillable worksheet. I'll circulate to help."

Then: "You have 20 minutes. Go."

Audience does (20 min)

Works in table groups. Fills in the worksheet on their phones. Uses the tool tables in the Workshop Guide to select tools. Discusses, decides, writes.

Speaker does (during the 20 min)

Circulate among tables. Coach stuck groups with seed ideas ("opioid stewardship conversations," "end-of-life discussions," "pediatric dosing"). Push back on cost ("Can this be done with a free tool?"). Watch for the VR trap. Project a visible countdown.

Slide 13Share-Out0:48–0:52 (4 min)
Speaker says

"Let's hear from 2 groups. 60–90 seconds each, what's your educational need, what tool did you pick, and why? What's one barrier and how will you handle it?"

Pick groups with diverse tool choices, one text/audio, one AR/VR, for maximum cross-pollination.

Audience does

2 groups share their plans. Others listen and ask questions.

Slide 14Wrap-Up0:52–0:53 (1 min)
Speaker says

"Everything you saw today is in the Workshop Guide, bookmark it. Share it with your colleagues. Start with one tool, one need, one small experiment. You don't need a grant, an IT ticket, or a technology expert. You need to know what your learners need, and you already know that."

Slide 15Thank You0:53–0:54 (1 min)
Speaker does

Display contact info. Stay for questions. Thank the audience.

Key Messages to Reinforce

The Five Things Attendees Should Remember
  1. You don't need to be a technology expert. You need to be a clinician who knows what your learners need.
  2. Ground AI in your source material. Upload your guidelines. Say "base your response only on the attached documents." NotebookLM does this for free.
  3. Authentic assessment, not MCQs. Use AI to create clinical reasoning cases, simulated encounters, and reflective prompts that require the learner to demonstrate their thinking.
  4. Simulation is a spectrum. Text-and-audio is not "less than" VR, it's different. Match the fidelity to the learning goal.
  5. Start with one tool, one need, one small experiment. You don't need a grant or an IT ticket. You need to know what your learners need, and you already know that.
