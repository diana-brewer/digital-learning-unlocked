Audience does (20 min)

Works in table groups. Fills in the worksheet on their phones. Uses the tool tables in the Workshop Guide to select tools. Discusses, decides, writes.

Speaker does (during the 20 min)

Circulate among tables. Coach stuck groups with seed ideas ("opioid stewardship conversations," "end-of-life discussions," "pediatric dosing"). Push back on cost ("Can this be done with a free tool?"). Watch for the VR trap. Announce time checks at 5, 10, 15, 18 minutes.

NotebookLM Setup (5 Minutes, Day Before)

  1. Go to notebooklm.google.com (free, Google account)
  2. Create a new notebook called "Pericarditis Teaching Case"
  3. Upload 2-3 source documents (I can provide a pericarditis guideline summary and case notes)
  4. Wait for processing (~30 sec)
  5. Test: "Create a clinical reasoning case of acute pericarditis based on the uploaded sources"
  6. Click "Audio Overview" — let it generate. Have it ready to play 15 seconds.
  7. Leave the notebook open on your laptop for the live demo.

Key Messages to Reinforce

The Five Things Attendees Should Remember
  1. You don't need to be a technology expert. You need to be a clinician who knows what your learners need.
  2. Ground AI in your source material. Upload your guidelines. Say "base your response only on the attached documents." NotebookLM does this for free.
  3. Authentic assessment, not MCQs. Use AI to create clinical reasoning cases, simulated encounters, and reflective prompts that require the learner to demonstrate their thinking.
  4. Simulation is a spectrum. Text-and-audio is not "less than" VR — it's different. Match the fidelity to the learning goal.
  5. Start with one tool, one need, one small experiment. You don't need a grant or an IT ticket. You need to know what your learners need — and you already know that.
